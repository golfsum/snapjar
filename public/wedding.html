Why couples do this

After the wedding, the photos scatter. Some are in a group chat, some on your aunt's phone, some on a camera roll you will never see. Asking people to send them afterward almost never works.

A QR code on the table catches the photos the night they happen, while everyone is still in the room and still excited. It also gives guests without a plus-one something fun to do, which is a real and underrated benefit.

Questions people actually ask

Do wedding guests need to download an app?

No. They scan the QR code with their phone camera. The album opens in the browser and they add photos from there.

Where should we put the QR code?

Reception tables first. Also the bar, the guestbook table, and a sign near the dance floor. The more people see it, the more photos you get.

Will this replace our photographer?

No. Your photographer still shoots the ceremony, portraits, and the planned moments. Snapjar collects the guest candids they were never going to catch.

Can faraway family watch the album?

Yes. Send them the album link. Photos appear live as guests add them, which is a kind way to include people who could not travel.
